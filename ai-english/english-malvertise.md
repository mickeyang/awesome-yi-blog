People occasionally use "malvertise" as a verb meaning "to spread malware via ads", but it's rarer and more informal. I'll teach you both so you're covered.

Pronunciation

malvertising: /ˌmæl.vərˈtaɪ.zɪŋ/ (US) — sounds like "mal-ver-TIE-zing"
Think of it as "malicious" + "advertising" smashed together.
malvertise (verb): /ˌmæl.vərˈtaɪz/ — "mal-ver-TIZE"

Tiny Concept

Malvertising is a portmanteau — a blend of "malicious" and "advertising". It refers to the technique where cybercriminals disguise malware inside online ads that look perfectly normal. The scary part: you don't even need to click the ad. Simply loading the webpage can trigger an infection (this is called a "drive-by download") [citation:7][citation:9].

It differs from "ad malware" — that term refers to malware targeting the ad network itself, while malvertising targets the end user through the ad [citation:9].

Examples in Context

1. "Security researchers detected a wave of malvertising on several high-traffic news sites, infecting visitors' computers without a single click."
2. "The cybercriminal attempted to malvertise through a popular ad exchange, hiding exploit kits inside banner ads."
3. "Major publishers like The New York Times and Yahoo have unknowingly served malvertising in the past" [citation:10].

Notice in example 2, I used "malvertise" as a verb — it works, but "engage in malvertising" would sound more natural to native ears.

A little nuance: "malvertising" is the method (the attack strategy), while "adware" is the result (the software that ends up on your machine) [citation:2]. So you'd say "They used malvertising to install adware."
