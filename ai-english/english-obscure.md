The word **“obscure”** has a few closely related meanings, but they all revolve around the idea of something being **not clear, not well known, or hard to understand or see**.

---

## 1. Meaning (core idea)

**obscure** = difficult to see, understand, or know about

It can be used as:

- **Adjective**: not famous / not well known / not clear  
- **Verb**: to hide or make something unclear

---

## 2. Common meanings with examples

### A. Not well known (very common in daily English)
Used for people, places, ideas, or things that are not famous.

- *He is an obscure writer who few people know.*
- *That band is still pretty obscure outside their hometown.*
- *The movie was based on an obscure novel from the 1980s.*

👉 Here, “obscure” means **low popularity or little recognition**

---

### B. Difficult to understand (ideas, explanations, language)
Used when something is unclear or complicated.

- *The instructions were so obscure that nobody understood them.*
- *Her explanation was obscure and confusing.*
- *The meaning of that sentence is obscure.*

👉 Here, it means **unclear or hard to interpret**

---

### C. Hidden / not easy to see (less common but still used)
Used when something is physically or visually hidden.

- *Clouds obscured the view of the mountains.*
- *Smoke obscured the road ahead.*

👉 Here, it means **blocking visibility**

---

## 3. As a verb (to obscure = to hide or make unclear)

- *Fog obscured the city skyline.*
- *He tried to obscure the truth.*
- *The details were obscured by too much technical language.*

---

## 4. Everyday usage summary

In daily English, you will most often hear:

- **“obscure artist / band / book”** → not famous  
- **“obscure meaning / explanation”** → hard to understand  
- **“obscure the view”** → block sight  

---

## 5. Quick memory trick

Think:

> **obscure = “not clear + not visible + not famous”**
