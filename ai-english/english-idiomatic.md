### 📘 Meaning

**Idiomatic** (adjective) means:

> **Using expressions, grammar, or vocabulary that sound natural to native speakers of a language.**

It often describes language that follows the *typical, natural patterns* of how people actually speak, rather than being technically correct but unnatural.

---

### 🧠 Key idea

- ✅ **Idiomatic English** = natural, fluent, like a native speaker  
- ❌ **Non-idiomatic English** = grammatically correct but sounds strange or awkward  

---

### 💬 Examples in Daily Life

#### 1. Talking about language ability
- *Her English is very **idiomatic**—you’d think she grew up in London.*

#### 2. Comparing natural vs unnatural phrasing
- ❌ *I very like this movie.* (not idiomatic)  
- ✅ *I really like this movie.* (idiomatic)

#### 3. Using idiomatic expressions
- *“Break the ice” is an **idiomatic** expression—it doesn’t mean breaking real ice.*

#### 4. In professional or academic contexts
- *Your writing is clear, but try to make it more **idiomatic**.*

---

### ✍️ Quick Tip

If you want to sound more **idiomatic**, focus on:
- Common phrases (collocations): *make a decision*, not *do a decision*
- Natural word order
- Everyday expressions used by native speakers
