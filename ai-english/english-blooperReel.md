Word: blooper reel
Date: 2026-09-06
Primary meaning: a video compilation of funny mistakes (flubbed lines, forgotten dialogue, props failing, actors breaking character...) made during the filming of a movie, TV show, or other production; often shown during end credits or as a DVD/Blu-ray bonus feature
Common synonyms: gag reel, outtakes
---

Pronunciation

- UK: /ˈbluː.pə ˌriːl/  ("BLOO-puh reel")
- US: /ˈbluː.pɚ ˌriːl/  ("BLOO-per reel")

Two syllables in blooper (bloop-er), one syllable in reel.

Tiny concept: what do the two words mean?

A blooper is a funny mistake — usually an actor forgetting lines, laughing during a serious scene, tripping, or a prop malfunctioning.

A reel originally meant a roll of film. So a blooper reel literally means "a roll of film containing the funny mistakes".

The phrase is used mostly in film and TV contexts. As a learner, you will most often hear it in sentences like:

- The blooper reel at the end of the movie had everyone laughing.
- The DVD extras include a blooper reel and a behind-the-scenes documentary.
- Pixar films are famous for their animated blooper reels during the credits.

Synonyms and related terms

- gag reel —完全同义, 更口语化, 好莱坞常用
- outtakes — 指被剪掉的镜头, 不一定是搞笑的, 但经常包含 bloopers
- behind-the-scenes / BTS — 相关概念, 但侧重幕后花絮, 不一定是失误

How to use it naturally

Here is how native speakers actually use the phrase:

1. As the subject: The blooper reel stole the show.
2. After prepositions (on, in, during): We stayed until the end of the credits to watch the blooper reel.
3. In the phrase "included as a bonus": The deluxe edition comes with a blooper reel.

Note: In British English, people sometimes just say "outtakes" or call funny mistakes a "cock-up" or "bloomer", but blooper reel is universally understood thanks to American films and DVDs.
