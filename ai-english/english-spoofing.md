Alright, let's look at "spoofing". It comes from the verb "spoof", which originally meant "hoax" or "light-hearted trick" (invented by a British comedian in the 1880s). Today it lives a double life — one serious, one funny.

Pronunciation: /spuːfɪŋ/ — "spoof" rhymes with roof / proof. The "-ing" is soft, one syllable total feel: SPOOF-ing.

## Two main senses

**1. Tech / cybersecurity (very common now)**
Someone sends a packet, email, or phone call that looks like it's coming from somewhere else.

- The attacker used IP spoofing to make the traffic look like it came from inside the company network.
- That call said it was from the bank, but it was caller ID spoofing — a scam.

**2. Comedy / media (the older sense)**
A spoof is a silly version of something well-known.

- Airplane! is a famous spoof of disaster movies.
- He wrote a spoof of that viral video, and it got a million views.
