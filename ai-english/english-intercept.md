Hi. I see you asked about the meaning of intercept in geometry. Good question. In mathematics, especially in coordinate geometry, intercept has a specific meaning.

Pronunciation: /ˌɪntərˈsept/ (same as before).

Tiny concept explanation:
In math, an intercept is a point where a line or a curve crosses one of the axes on a graph. The most common ones are the x-intercept and the y-intercept.
- The x-intercept is the point where the line crosses the x-axis. At that point, the y-coordinate is 0.
- The y-intercept is the point where the line crosses the y-axis. At that point, the x-coordinate is 0.
As a verb, to intercept can mean to cut off or intersect, but as a noun it is the crossing point.

Examples in context:
1. The line y = 2x + 4 has a y-intercept at (0, 4).
2. To find the x-intercept, set y = 0 and solve for x.
3. The graph shows two intercepts: one on the x-axis and one on the y-axis.
4. In geometry, we say the line intercepts the x-axis at x = 3.
