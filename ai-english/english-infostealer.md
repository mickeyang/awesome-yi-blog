Pronunciation

American: /ˈɪn foʊˌstil ər/ — "IN-foh-steel-er"
It is a compound noun made from "info" + "stealer". The stress falls on the first syllable.

Meaning

An infostealer is a type of malware. Its only job is to secretly collect sensitive data from your computer or phone — things like saved passwords, browser cookies, autofill data, cryptocurrency wallet files, and login tokens. Once it grabs the data, it sends everything back to the attacker.

In plain English: it is a digital thief that lives inside your browser.

How it works (simple version)

The infostealer arrives → it reads your browser's saved passwords and cookies → it packs the data into a "log" → it sends the log to the attacker → it disappears. The whole thing can finish in seconds, often without you noticing.

2. Security researchers found that the infostealer had quietly exported over 10,000 saved passwords from the victim's Chrome browser.
   （安全研究人员发现，该信息窃取器已悄悄从受害者的 Chrome 浏览器导出了逾 1 万组保存的密码。）

3. Using a dedicated password manager instead of saving passwords in your browser is one of the best defenses against infostealers.
   （使用专门的密码管理器、而不是把密码存在浏览器里，是对抗信息窃取器的最佳防御手段之一。）

4. The stolen data is usually packaged into "steler logs" and sold on underground markets.
   （被盗数据通常被打包成"窃取日志"，在地下市场出售。）

Grammar note

"Infostealer" is a countable noun. Its plural is infostealers.
- one infostealer / two infostealers
You can use it with articles: an infostealer, the infostealer.

Native phrasing tips

In cybersecurity articles, you will often see:
- "infostealer malware" (redundant but common)
- "stoler log" (the packaged stolen data)
- "an infostealer infection"
Avoid saying "an info stealer malware" — pick one form: either "an infostealer" or "information stealer malware".
